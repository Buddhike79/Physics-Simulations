What the simulation is illustrating

n-type

Group V atoms contribute an extra electron that does not participate in the four covalent bonds. These are donor atoms; electrons are the majority carriers and holes are the minority carriers.

p-type

Group III atoms leave one bond short of an electron. The vacancy is represented as a hole. These are acceptor atoms; holes are the majority carriers and electrons are the minority carriers.

Electrical neutrality

The crystal remains electrically neutral overall. Doping increases mobile carrier concentration without making the entire crystal carry a net charge.

More doping

Under normal doping conditions, the majority-carrier concentration becomes much greater than the intrinsic carrier concentration, so conductivity rises and resistivity falls.

Temperature

Heating creates additional electron–hole pairs by thermal agitation. In an extrinsic semiconductor this strongly affects the minority carriers, while the dopant-created majority carriers dominate under normal conditions.

Not a microscopic calculation

The counts and motion are a visual teaching model. They are designed to show relationships described in the textbook, not to reproduce a real crystal atom-for-atom.
