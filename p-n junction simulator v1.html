What the simulator is teaching

1. Formation of the junction

Majority holes diffuse p → n and majority electrons diffuse n → p. Near the junction they recombine.

2. Fixed ions

The p-side depletion region is shown with fixed negative acceptor ions. The n-side depletion region is shown with fixed positive donor ions.

3. Internal field and barrier

The separated fixed charges create an internal electric field from the n side toward the p side and an internal potential barrier (Vb). The book gives about 0.7 V for Si and 0.3 V for Ge at room temperature.

4. Reverse bias

p is connected to the negative terminal and n to the positive terminal. Majority carriers are driven away from the junction, so the depletion region widens and the barrier increases to (Vb + Es). The main model treats the junction current as zero.

5. Forward bias

p is positive and n is negative. Majority carriers approach the depletion region. If the applied voltage is still below the barrier, they do not cross and there is no appreciable current. At or above the barrier, they cross and a large forward current appears.

6. Minority carriers

Animation convention: Minority carriers are drawn small; when a minority carrier crosses into the other semiconductor, it is redrawn large because it is now a majority carrier there.

Minority carriers cross the depletion region. An electron moving p → n becomes a majority electron in the n-region. A hole moving n → p becomes a majority hole in the p-region. They are then swept toward the appropriate contact and do not return through the junction.
