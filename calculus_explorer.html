Archimedes' Workshop

How can we find the area of a curved region without calculus? Archimedes used geometric reasoning to trap an unknown area between known areas. This family of techniques is called the method of exhaustion.

From Sums to Integrals

Integration can be understood as the limit of a sum of many small contributions. Each rectangle contributes approximately its height multiplied by its width.

Area ≈ Σ f(xᵢ*) Δx

For equal-width rectangles, Δx = (b − a)/n. As the number of rectangles increases, their width decreases. For a suitable function, the sums approach a definite integral:

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = limₙ→∞ Σ f(xᵢ*) Δx
Connect this to the graph

Return to Explore Functions. Change n and watch the shaded rectangles and error graph update. The exact integral is the limiting signed sum, not necessarily the total geometric area.

Three ideas to remember

  1. Break a difficult region into simple pieces.
  2. Add the contributions to estimate the whole.
  3. Refine the pieces and study the limit to define the exact area.
