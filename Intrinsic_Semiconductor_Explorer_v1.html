What should students notice?

1. Increase temperature

More bonds are broken in the animation, so more electron–hole pairs appear.

2. Electrons and holes appear together

Each broken bond produces one free electron and one hole, so the simulated counts track each other.

3. Generation and recombination

Pairs can disappear again as a free electron recombines into a hole, illustrating dynamic equilibrium.

4. Silicon vs germanium

At 300 K the resource book gives different intrinsic carrier concentrations: Si ≈ 1.5×1010 cm−3, Ge ≈ 2.5×1013 cm−3.

5. Larger crystal

The crystal-size slider increases the number of bonds shown; it changes the size of the visual sample, not the physics per bond.

6. No doping here

This explorer is intentionally for an intrinsic (pure) semiconductor. Majority/minority carriers are introduced later when doping is studied.

Resource basis: Grade 13 Physics, Unit 09, Chapter 1. The simulator is a qualitative teaching model. The carrier-count animation is not a calibrated microscopic calculation; the book's stated 300 K Si/Ge concentrations are shown as reference values.
